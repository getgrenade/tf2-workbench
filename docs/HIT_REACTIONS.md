# Play mode hit reactions

Settings -> Play mode hit reactions controls the feature immediately (enabled by default). A bullet or melee hit ragdolls the first character in its path. Rockets and live pipes collide with characters; rocket/pipe/cannon explosions and armed sticky detonations gib nearby characters within the weapon script blast radius. Jumper weapons with zero damage do not gib. Both teams can be hit in this preview; each hit is treated as fatal without health/damage accumulation. Arrows, syringes and energy rings stop on character hits but do not cause these explosive gib reactions.

Character collision uses the original model first hitbox set transformed by its current posed bones, with swept segments to prevent fast projectiles tunneling. Posed mesh bounds are a fallback when hitbox metadata is unavailable. Blast overlap uses the Source player collision hull (see Performance of crowds). The shooter is excluded; already affected characters cannot be killed repeatedly or healed. Gib assets load only when first needed from mounted VPK data.

Play's left action list also includes 1 Ragdoll, 2 Explode (the controlled character), 8 Reset damage (all affected characters), and 9 Damage ON/OFF. They are buttons and keyboard shortcuts, alongside the unchanged 3-6 gestures. OFF takes effect immediately, restores existing reactions and disables manual/weapon damage; the setting is saved through a serialized queue so rapid toggles retain the latest choice. Movement, firing and asynchronous animation writes pause while the controlled character is damaged, while reset/toggle controls remain available. Reset restores the character and Play resumes; exiting keeps its physics pose for screenshots.

## Source behavior used

- game/client/tf/c_tf_player.cpp: CreatePlayerGibs adds Z1 to incoming velocity, normalizes, multiplies by force500, caps at400; pieces get random X/Y angular impulse in 0-120 degrees/sec.
- game/shared/props_shared.cpp: BuildGibList reads repeated break blocks in class PHY keyvalues. CreateGibsFromList uses original model paths, posed placement bones / placementOrigin, offsets, velocity overrides, inherited skin, random direction and burst impulse.
- Original class PHY lists resolve 10 Scout, 10 Soldier, 9 Pyro, 7 Demoman, 16 Heavy, 8 Engineer, 9 Medic, 8 Sniper and 8 Spy pieces in the installed game. Geometry and attachment readers accept MDL44-48; gib geometry uses skinned bind transforms without requiring the MDL48 animation decoder.

## Blood effects

Bullet and melee ragdoll hits dispatch `blood_impact_red_01` at the trace impact position and `blood_spray_red_01` / `_far` according to camera distance (400-unit cutoff). Explosive gib victims dispatch the same damage blood at their posed body centre, oriented away from the explosion. These are original VPK PCFs, materials and textures, simulated by the existing Source helper; no blood textures are bundled or synthesized.

`game/server/tf/tf_player.cpp` sends TE_TFBlood for bleeding damage. `game/client/tf/tf_fx_blood.cpp` orients impact against the damage normal and spray along it, pushing view-aligned spray sideways based on camera direction/distance. The viewer implements this normal-violence, dry-world path. Normal `CreateTFGibs` does not add a universal blood explosion/trail; its special sniper mist, birthday/confetti and burning effects are separate conditions and are not added here. Underwater, low-violence, Pyro Vision and birthday replacements remain outside this preview.

Blood follows the existing hit-reaction setting and lifecycle. Exiting Play stops its native helpers immediately and keeps the current particle frame for screenshots, with camera-facing draw updates. Reset/new Play, disabling reactions, deleting a victim or disposing the scene releases helpers/draw resources; late async creation cannot attach after reset. Live bursts clean up when the native graph is empty, with a 10-second safety limit. Blood/death previews are not saved in scene files. Original PCF/material assets share the in-memory particle cache; only required blood graphs/materials are prewarmed when Play prepares hit reactions.

## Viewer physics and lifecycle

Ragdolls and gibs share a fixed 120Hz articulated simulation. The new `model_physics` command reads original PHY solids (bone/mass), joint angle limits and self-collision rules from mounted VPKs. Collision pairs ENABLE contact, as in `ragdoll_shared.cpp`; absent rules default to nonadjacent body contact. Helpers, fingers and facial bones follow their nearest physical ancestor rather than falling independently. Approximate capsule self contacts and mesh-derived floor support keep bodies from folding through each other. Joint anchors use bind bone-to-bone transforms, while simulation starts in the currently displayed pose.

Gib models are skinned against their simulated bones. Their centre of mass and floor support come from the actual geometry, fixing models whose vertices are far above the model origin. Single-solid older gibs can have a PHY reference name different from their sole render bone; that body maps to the root. Single-body debris retains launch spin with time-based damping. Ground contact uses actual mesh support vertices, normal/bounce impulses and friction impulses with approximate inertia, transferring sliding motion into tumble/rolling instead of multiplying speed down each tick. Coplanar support vertices are averaged so flat faces can settle, with gentle rolling resistance and sleeping. Multi-solid pieces use the same constrained solver. Metadata is cached in memory on demand; reset disposes skeletons and debris geometry.

Melee reads script SmackDelay (default 0.2 seconds), standard range48 / sword range72 and hull18, plus item range/bounds multipliers. At strike time it samples current aim, traces the central ray first, then a swept hull from the shoot position. Larger character scale expands range/hull; smaller scale keeps standard reach. Weapon switching or ending play cancels pending strikes. Fists work without requiring muzzle/particle geometry. `tf_weaponbase_melee.cpp` is the reference.

This is **not native Source VPhysics**: original convex hulls/inertia, exact joint friction/contact impulses, world/prop/body contacts between separate ragdolls are not reproduced. Wearable/weapon break models, violence/birthday substitutions, damage-dependent gib thresholds, spread/pellet patterns and Source fade timing are not implemented. Hitscan currently traces the central aim ray once per fired round.

Exiting Play freezes reactions for screenshots. A new Play session, disabling the setting, removing a character or disposing the scene releases debris geometry and restores original animation/pose state. Scene saving keeps the original character pose and playback rather than overwriting it with temporary reactions; debris/death states are not persisted. Articulated bodies sleep after settling or at a 20-second preview limit. The fallback skeleton used when physics metadata is unavailable stops after 12 seconds. Character metadata and VPK assets use memory caches. No gib assets are bundled or written to a persistent gib cache.

Validation: focused JS checks include delayed/cancelled melee strikes, shoot-position/hull tracing, all nine classes retaining joint connections/angular bounds, and all 85 installed gib pieces falling with finite transforms and floor support. Debris checks verify retained airborne spin, impact-induced rolling without launch spin, and stable sleep of a flat piece. A synthetic offset-origin gib verifies visible geometry reaches the floor and sleeping is independent of render FPS. Existing hit/play/projectile/healing/snapshot regressions pass. Installed backend checks export/validate all class and gib physics; melee profiles, frontend and dev backend builds pass. Blood validation adds Source direction/LOD/lifecycle checks; all three original blood graphs emit finite native particles and finish within the cleanup window. Live visual comparison remains pending. Restart the development client for the new command; regenerate portable output with build.bat.

## Performance of crowds (2026-10-08)

A rocket into many characters used to stall and spawn effects late. What changed, and what each fix is based on:

- **Blast victims** are found against the Source player collision hull (-24..24 by 0..82 around the feet, scaled with the model; `tf_gamerules.cpp` `g_TFViewVectors`), as `RadiusDamage` does with `CollisionProp()->CalcNearestPoint`. Before, every character's whole mesh was skinned on the CPU to get bounds, several times per victim. The direction and blood position use the hull centre too (`WorldSpaceCenter`).
- **Blood** no longer starts two native helper processes per victim (about 34 MB and 80 ms of CPU each). `BloodPool` (particles/blood.ts) keeps one helper per blood system (impact, spray, far spray) with 16 collections that restart on their action flag, like the projectile impact pools; hits are free after the first. An explosion sends blood for the nearest eight victims. `installed_blood_pools` (native_particles.rs) checks independent origins, expiry and restart.
- **Gibs** load once into a shared `GibAsset` (mesh geometry, materials, attachments, physics, skeleton) instead of per victim, the pieces of a burst load in parallel, and `prepare(load)` preloads the gibs of every character in the scene when Play prepares its weapon, so the burst appears in one frame. Before, pieces loaded one after another (three round trips each) while the character kept standing.
- **Ragdoll solver** (ragdollPhysics.ts): no allocation per 120 Hz step; results equal the old solver to 1e-13 on all nine class ragdolls and the installed gibs (checked with a copy of the old class), about 2.2x faster; body pairs whose bounding spheres do not touch are skipped (exact, since a capsule lies inside that sphere); the vertex scan that sizes each solid is cached per model; a frame runs at most four fixed steps; with many awake articulated bodies each gets fewer solver iterations (24, then 16 above four, 10 above eight).
- **Particle sprites and ropes** (particles/render.ts, rope.ts): the per-frame update allocates nothing per particle; the attribute data it writes is identical.

Not changed, and why: the extra scene depth pass for soft particles still renders the whole scene every frame while a soft-depth effect is visible (swapping materials for a cheap one would change alpha-tested depth); ragdoll joint friction (PHY `friction` is parsed but unused), ragdoll/gib fade (`cl_ragdoll_fade_time` 15 s in `c_tf_player.cpp`), the death push (`m_vecRagdollVelocity` plus the damage force, not a fixed 180/70), victim velocity at death, and ragdoll-versus-world contacts remain approximations.

## Blood on the ground (2026-10-09)

TF2 gib pieces are client-side physics props (C_PhysPropClientside, BreakModelCreateSingle). A piece whose model keyvalues contain world_interactions { "onworldimpact" "bloodsplat" } (61 of the 87 installed gib models, e.g. most Soldier, Sniper and Spy pieces) shoots the "Blood" decal group when it touches the world: StartTouch (throttled to once per 0.1 s) calls HitSurface, which calls UTIL_BloodDecalTrace (physpropclientside.cpp, util_shared.cpp). The group (scripts/decals_subrect.txt) is six "Subrect" materials decals/blood1_subrect..blood6_subrect, each a position and size in the decals/decals_mod2x atlas (1024x512, DecalModulate: surface * 2 * decal, so mid grey leaves it alone) with $decalscale 0.25 (128 px = 32 units).

Implemented: parseParticleModel flags bloodSplat by finding the word in the MDL; ArticulatedRagdoll.onImpact reports where a body lands on the floor (once per landing, at most every 0.1 s); a gib piece with the flag calls HitReactions.decals().add(point, up); BloodDecals (particles/decals.ts) keeps up to 100 floor-aligned quads (oldest reused), random variant and rotation, modulate-2x blending (the scene is linear, so the decal is linearised and scaled by 2^2.2 / 2), cleared with the reactions. The atlas and the six sub-rectangles are read from the game at run time; only the six material names are fixed (from the script). Decals are preloaded when Play prepares its weapon.

Not done: decals on walls (the preview has only a floor and an invisible box, and gibs report floor landings only), gib fade-out (the fadeTime of the break definition, then 3 s of fade in the game), and any decal for bullet or melee ragdolls (the game leaves none).
